Revenue Leak Detection: Where Pipeline Value Quietly Disappears

Pipeline leakage rarely announces itself as a single failed deal. It appears in the gaps between stages, people and decisions.

Kartik Clarity™ · September 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Pipeline leakage is often invisible because every individual stage can appear busy. Leads are being generated. Calls are happening. Proposals are being sent. Yet expected revenue still fails to arrive.

The five leakage moments

The source architecture identifies five revenue leak zones: Silent Zone, Trust Gap, Follow-Up Graveyard, Handoff Fracture and Renewal Blind Spot. Together they describe where buyer intent can weaken before the business notices.

  • Silent Zone: interested prospects become unresponsive without a useful recovery signal.
  • Trust Gap: buyer confidence falls before the process catches it.
  • Follow-Up Graveyard: a warm opportunity receives too little or too-late follow-up.
  • Handoff Fracture: critical context is lost between selling and delivery.
  • Renewal Blind Spot: risk becomes visible after the decision is effectively made.

Why handoffs matter

Every handoff creates a potential information loss. A buyer should not have to repeat the same problem, priority or expected outcome because ownership changed internally.

Prioritize by economic impact

Not every leak deserves the same response. Rank issues by revenue at risk, frequency, recoverability and speed to intervention. Fix the leak that changes the economics first.

Build the feedback loop

A diagnostic should not end with a score. Connect the finding to a specific intervention, then verify whether the expected revenue behavior improved.
